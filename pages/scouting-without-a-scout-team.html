Scouting Smarter: Preparing for Any Opponent Without a Scout Team

You do not need a full scout team to give your players a real look at Friday's opponent.

The Coaches' Box · July 2026 · 5 min read

Ask any coach what steals the most preparation from their week and a lot of them will land on the same answer: the scout team. You need bodies who can line up in the other guys' formations and run their plays well enough to teach against. Most programs simply do not have enough of them. Backups are learning their own assignments, JV kids get pulled up and pulled back, and the scout cards get run half speed and out of alignment. Your starters end up preparing against a look that will not resemble Friday at all.

I stopped treating the scout team as the only way to show my players an opponent. Here is the approach that has served me best when I am short on bodies.

Start With Tendencies, Not Just Plays

Before I worry about running anything, I figure out what my opponent actually does. What do they line up in on first down. What is their favorite third and long pressure. Which coverage shows up in the red zone. When I boil a team down to the six or eight looks they truly live in, I can prepare my players for the vast majority of Friday without chasing every play in the playbook.

Show the Look Before You Rep It

Recognition comes first, execution comes second. I want my quarterback and my linemen to see a formation and know what it means before the ball is ever snapped. So I show the look, name it, and let players call out what they expect. Once they can recognize it standing still, the physical rep goes far faster and far cleaner.

Make the Cards You Do Run Count

If I only have the bodies for a handful of quality scout reps, I refuse to waste them. I would rather run ten looks aligned perfectly than forty looks run sloppy. A scout rep that is out of alignment teaches the wrong picture, and the wrong picture is worse than no picture at all. Quality over quantity, every time.

Let Technology Fill the Gaps

This is where the game has changed for coaches at every level. You no longer need eleven extra bodies to show your players an opponent. You can project the exact formations and coverages a team runs and let every defender take a clean mental rep of that look, again and again. The Bottom Line

A great scout team is a luxury most programs cannot count on. Preparation is not. Study your opponent down to their real tendencies, teach recognition before execution, protect the quality of every rep you run, and lean on tools that let you show any look without needing the bodies. Do that and your players will walk onto the field Friday having already seen what is coming.
