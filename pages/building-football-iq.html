Building Football IQ: Teaching Players to See the Game

Recognition is a skill you can coach. Here is how I build players who diagnose the play before the snap.

The Coaches' Box · July 2026 · 5 min read

We spend a lot of time in this profession chasing speed. Faster feet, faster hands, faster forty times. Speed matters, but I will take a player who sees the game a half second early over a faster player who reacts late every single time. Football IQ, the ability to diagnose what is happening and respond before the play fully develops, is the difference between a good player and a great one. And here is the part a lot of coaches miss. It is a skill you can teach.

Recognition is not a gift some kids are born with and others are not. It is trained, the same way footwork is trained. Here is how I build it.

Teach the Why, Not Just the What

A player who only knows his assignment is helpless the moment something looks different. A player who knows why he has that assignment can adjust on the fly. So I never hand out a job without explaining the reason behind it. When a player understands how his role fits the whole picture, he stops guessing and starts anticipating.

Rep Recognition, Not Just Assignments

Most practice reps train the body. I make sure some of them train the eyes. I will show a formation and have players call out what they expect before anyone moves. I will flash a coverage and ask my quarterback where the ball should go. The more times a player correctly identifies a look in practice, the faster he will identify it under the lights.

Give Players Pre-Snap Keys

Great recognition is not magic. It is knowing where to look. I teach my players a small number of reliable pre-snap keys, the tells that tip off what is coming. Alignment, splits, depth, body language. When a player knows his key and trusts it, the game slows down for him because he is reading the right thing instead of staring at everything.

Quiz on the Fly

I am constantly asking my players to explain what they just saw. What was that coverage. Why did the play work. What would you do if they shifted. These are thirty second questions, but they force a player to process the game instead of just moving through it. Over a season, that habit of thinking turns into instinct.

The Bottom Line

The best players see the game a beat before everyone else, and that beat can be coached. Teach the why behind every assignment, rep recognition on purpose, give players reliable keys to read, and keep asking them to explain what they see. Build those habits and you will develop players who do not just run their assignment. They understand the game, and that is what carries them long after the final whistle.
