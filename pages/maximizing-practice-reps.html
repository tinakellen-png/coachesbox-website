How to Get More Out of Every Practice Rep When Time Is Short

Coach to coach, here is how I stopped wasting the clock and started building real learning.

The Coaches' Box · May 2026 · 6 min read

Every season I hear the same thing from coaches, and I feel it myself. There is never enough time. The practice window keeps shrinking, the schedule keeps filling up, and Friday night keeps coming whether we are ready or not. For a long time I tried to solve that problem by moving faster. I crammed more into every period and rushed from drill to drill. What I learned the hard way is that speed is not the same as productivity. A rep only counts if a player learns from it.

So I flipped my thinking. Instead of asking how many reps I could fit into practice, I started asking how much learning I could get out of each one. That single change did more for my teams than any new drill ever did. Here is exactly how I approach it now.

Plan Backward From the Rep, Not Forward From the Schedule

Most of us build a practice plan by filling time slots. We know we have two hours, so we chop it into periods and drop drills into the boxes. The problem is that the schedule becomes the goal instead of the learning. Now I start at the other end. I write down the three or four things my players absolutely must be able to do by Friday, and then I build every period around those outcomes. If a drill does not serve one of those outcomes, it does not make the plan. It is amazing how much clutter disappears when you plan this way.

Protect the First Ten Minutes

The freshest minds of the entire practice happen right at the start. That is when I install the newest or hardest concept, not stretching or busy work. I want players learning the toughest material while they are sharp, not when they are tired and the sun is going down. If you are teaching something brand new, teach it early and revisit it late so it has time to settle.

Talk Less, Show More, Rep Sooner

I used to explain a concept for five minutes before anyone moved. Players would nod, and then run it wrong the second the ball was snapped. Now I keep the explanation short, walk them through it once slowly, and then get them repping it right away. Understanding lives in the doing, not in the talking. The faster I get players moving through a concept correctly, the faster it becomes theirs.

Use Mental Reps to Multiply Physical Ones

This is the biggest unlock I have found. Not every player can get a live rep on every play, but every player can get a mental rep. While one group runs the play, the next group watches with intent and visualizes their own assignment. I coach the sideline just as hard as the field. When you teach players how to take a quality mental rep, you effectively double or triple the value of every snap. This is the exact philosophy behind our Virtual Practice Simulator, and it started right here on the grass.

End Every Period With a Quick Check

Before we move on, I ask a couple of players to tell me what we just worked on and why. If they can explain it in their own words, it stuck. If they cannot, I know I need to circle back before we lose the day. This takes thirty seconds and saves me from discovering on Friday that a concept never landed.

The Bottom Line

You cannot add hours to your week, but you can add value to your reps. Plan backward from what players must learn, teach the hard stuff while minds are fresh, cut the talking, treat mental reps as real reps, and check for understanding before you move on. Do those five things and you will get more out of a short practice than most programs get out of a long one. That is not a shortcut. That is coaching smarter.
