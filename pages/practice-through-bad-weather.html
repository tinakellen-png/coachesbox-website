Rain, Heat, or Lightning: Keeping Your Team Sharp When You Can't Get on the Field

The forecast will steal practice days every season. Here is how to make sure it never steals your preparation.

The Coaches' Box · July 2026 · 5 min read

Every coach has lived it. The radar lights up, the horn sounds, and you are herding players inside with a game five days away. Or it is the first week of August and the heat index makes a full pads practice a bad idea. The forecast does not care about your install schedule. What separates prepared teams from the rest is not whether they lose field days. Everyone loses them. It is what they do with the day once the field is gone.

I decided a long time ago that a rained out practice would never mean a wasted one. Here is how I keep my team getting better when we cannot get outside.

Have an Indoor Plan Before You Need It

The worst time to design your bad weather practice is during the lightning delay. By then you are improvising in a loud gym with restless players. I build my indoor plan at the same time I build my field plan, every week. When the horn sounds, we do not scramble. We move to plan B and keep teaching without missing a beat.

Protect the Mental Reps

You cannot run full speed in a cafeteria, but you can absolutely teach recognition and assignments. A walkthrough in a hallway still builds the seeing that wins on Friday. I keep my players locked into formations, calls, and keys. The bodies are moving slow, but the minds are working at full speed, and that is where the real learning lives.

Turn Film Into Active Learning

Film time indoors is only valuable if players are working, not watching. I do not let them sit passively. I pause the clip and ask what they see. I have them call the coverage before the snap plays out. I put a player on the board to draw their assignment. When film becomes a conversation instead of a lecture, an indoor day can teach as much as a field day.

Keep the Bodies and the Minds Engaged

The fastest way to lose an indoor practice is to let it turn into standing around. I script it just as tightly as a field session. Short segments, clear objectives, everyone with a job. A projector or a screen changes everything here, because you can show the exact look you are teaching and run a live walkthrough right there in the room. That is a big part of why we built our simulator the way we did, so a weather day never has to be a lost day.

The Bottom Line

Weather is going to take field days away from you every single season. It does not have to take your preparation. Build the indoor plan before you need it, protect your mental reps, make film an active exercise, and keep every player engaged. Do that and the teams that lose their edge in bad weather will be the ones you beat on Friday.

Never lose a day to the forecast.

When you can't get on the field, project live walkthroughs on any screen and keep your players learning.
